I have a problem with note-taking apps. Specifically, I keep switching them. Over the past four years I've migrated my notes at least six times, each time convinced that the new tool would finally make me organized.

It didn't. The tool was never the problem.

## The pattern

It always goes the same way. I discover a new app, spend a weekend setting it up, import my old notes, build an elaborate folder structure, and feel productive for about two weeks. Then life gets busy, I stop maintaining the system, and three months later I'm taking notes in random text files again.

## What I learned

The system that works is the one you actually use when you're tired, distracted, and don't feel like being organized. For me, that turned out to be embarrassingly simple.

I use a single folder of markdown files. The filename is the date followed by a few words: `2026-02-19-project-ideas.md`. No tags, no backlinks, no graph view. When I need to find something, I use grep.

## But what about knowledge graphs?

I wanted to love Obsidian's graph view. It looks beautiful in screenshots. In practice, my graph was a meaningless blob of interconnected nodes that told me nothing I didn't already know. Backlinks are useful if you write a personal wiki. For daily notes and project scratchpads, they're overhead.

## The real lesson

Every hour spent organizing notes is an hour not spent doing the thing the notes are about. The perfect system is the one that gets out of your way fast enough that you forget it's there.

If Notion works for you, great. If a paper notebook works, also great. The only wrong answer is spending more time on the system than on the work.
