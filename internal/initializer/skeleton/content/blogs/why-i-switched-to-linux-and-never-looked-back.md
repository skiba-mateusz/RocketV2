I resisted Linux for a long time. Not because I thought it was bad — I just didn't see a reason to leave what worked. Then my MacBook died during a deadline, and I installed Ubuntu on an old ThinkPad out of desperation.

That was two years ago. I'm still here.

## The tipping point

It wasn't one thing. It was the accumulation of small frustrations: forced restarts for updates, bloatware I couldn't remove, a file manager that thought it knew better than me. Linux didn't solve all my problems, but it gave me the feeling that my computer was actually mine.

## What surprised me

Package managers changed how I think about software. Instead of googling for installers and clicking through wizards, I type one command. Need FFmpeg? `sudo apt install ffmpeg`. Done. No bundled toolbar, no "would you also like to install McAfee?"

The other surprise was how little I missed. VS Code works. Firefox works. Spotify works. The 2% of software that doesn't have a Linux version usually has an alternative that's good enough.

## What was hard

Printing. I'll be honest — setting up a printer on Linux took me an entire evening. Bluetooth was flaky for the first month until I found the right driver. And gaming, while much better than it used to be thanks to Proton, still requires occasional tinkering.

## Would I recommend it?

If you're a developer or a writer — someone who mostly lives in a browser and a terminal — absolutely. If you depend on Adobe Creative Suite or specific Windows-only software, probably not yet.

The best part isn't any single feature. It's the quiet confidence that nothing is happening on your machine that you didn't ask for.
